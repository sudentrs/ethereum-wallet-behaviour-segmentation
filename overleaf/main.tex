\begin{abstract}
Public blockchains expose detailed histories, but a partition of addresses does not by itself explain population behaviour. We evaluate Ethereum wallet segmentation through historical cohort controls, simple baselines and complementary network and temporal views. A complete year-end code scan retains 2,508,997 addresses from a supplied 2,738,243-address universe. Sixteen count/activity/direction features exclude pooled token amount magnitudes. Annual K2--K6 comparisons identify K3 as an interpretable working description, with three-seed ARI 0.908--0.971. However, twenty training-subset refits yield K3 ARI 0.257--0.986, while feature and estimator changes reveal substantial sensitivity. Non-self K3 network alignment survives monthly activity stratification in 1 of twelve months, but in 12 months after excluding the most active 1\% of endpoints, under a combined 300-test Holm correction. Graph composition therefore materially affects the interpretation. Frozen monthly models reveal active-only persistence and conditional return, while inactivity-aware transitions expose omitted participation paths. Separately fitted early-period K3 states improve later-month participation prediction over constant prevalence but underperform activity-feature baselines. Daily representations show partial cross-representation agreement, while histogram-preserving daily and block nulls detect sample-mean temporal ordering without establishing repeated cycles. The contribution is an auditable account of the evidence and counterevidence for a bounded behavioural summary, rather than permanent identities or a uniquely validated taxonomy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Public blockchains such as Ethereum expose transaction histories at a scale that makes detailed behavioural analysis possible. However, transparency does not automatically make an ecosystem understandable. A block explorer can reveal the activity of an individual address, while millions of transfer records still leave a population-level question unanswered: what patterns of participation recur, and how do those patterns change over time? Addresses with similar transfer totals can differ substantially in the duration, concentration, direction and timing of their activity. Summarizing these differences requires more than counting transactions or inspecting individual histories.

Wallet segmentation offers a compact vocabulary for describing a large address population. Prior studies demonstrate the informational value of Ethereum transaction histories for ownership linkage, profiling and behavioural classification \cite{victor2020address,beres2021blockchain,bonifazi2022userspectra}. Our target is observable address-level participation. We ask whether groups discovered without semantic labels provide a defensible description of behaviour, rather than attempting to infer who controls an address. Throughout this paper, ``wallet'' denotes an address-level analytical unit; an address is not assumed to represent one person or one organization.

The difficulty is that a clustering algorithm returns a partition even when its groups are unstable, depend heavily on the representation, or add little beyond simple activity thresholds. An interpretable name and a favourable internal score therefore do not establish a useful taxonomy. General clustering-validation research distinguishes internal separation, stability and substantive interpretation \cite{halkidi2001validation,liu2022stability}. For Ethereum segmentation, these distinctions lead to a concrete research problem: what evidence is needed before a set of unsupervised groups can be used to describe participation in the system?

Behaviour also depends on the observation window. A year-long history can summarize sustained participation or short-span concentration, while monthly observations reveal persistence, inactivity and movement between states. Temporal blockchain analysis motivates examining this evolution explicitly \cite{agarwal2021temporal}. We distinguish annual behavioural roles, independently learned monthly states, and daily time/frequency representations. Their numerical labels need not correspond, and agreement between representations must be measured rather than assumed.

We investigate this problem using the ERC-20 Trading 2022 release and its dataset description \cite{somin2025erc2022,somin2025dataset}. A complete historical code scan retains 2,508,997 addresses from a supplied 2,738,243-address feature universe. Membership requires empty code at the final Ethereum block of 2022. On this fixed year-end code-free cohort, we construct a sixteen-feature count/activity/direction representation and compare annual K2--K6 models. Pooled token amount magnitudes are excluded because they do not share a common economic unit. Annual K3 serves as the main descriptive segmentation, while competing resolutions remain part of the evidence.

The central research question is:
\begin{quote}
To what extent can unsupervised Ethereum address segments provide an interpretable and reproducible account of participation across aggregate features, transaction-network structure and temporal activity?
\end{quote}

The contribution is an auditable validation design that keeps these questions distinct. First, historical membership and feature controls establish what population and measurements the segmentation describes. Second, initialization, resampling, feature-family and algorithm comparisons identify which aspects of the annual partition are reproducible and which depend on modelling choices. Third, network randomization, inactivity-aware monthly evaluation and rebuilt daily signals examine how the descriptions relate to interaction and temporal structure. Simple segmentation and participation baselines assess whether clustering adds information beyond readily available activity measurements.

This design is useful because the evidence can disagree. Greater network alignment need not accompany stronger initialization stability; stable optimization need not imply stability to training subsets; and a significant timing statistic need not establish repeated cycles. The contribution is therefore a measured account of which behavioural descriptions survive which checks, rather than a claim that Ethereum contains a unique number of permanent wallet types.

The study is positioned as a measurement problem in a decentralized socio-technical system, not as an analysis of verified interpersonal ties. Ethereum records make observable how address-level participants enter, persist in and leave an interaction system, while the ownership and social meaning of those addresses remain unknown. The contribution to computational social-systems research is consequently methodological: it makes the scope, sensitivity and limits of a population-level behavioural summary explicit before that summary is used for broader social or operational claims.

\section{Motivation}
The practical motivation is to make large-scale participation understandable without requiring verified labels for every address. Individual histories answer questions about a particular wallet, but they do not summarize how a population is composed or how its activity changes. A compact behavioural description could support ecosystem monitoring and the formulation of more specific hypotheses about participation. Applications such as anomaly detection or agent-based simulation are potential downstream uses; they require their own validation.

Consider two addresses with the same annual number of transfers. One may spread its activity over several months, while the other concentrates almost all activity into a short episode. A third may transact more often but only on a few isolated days. Transfer totals alone combine these patterns. Duration, density, concentration and daily ordering can separate them, but adding features also changes the geometry used to form clusters. The resulting segments must therefore be evaluated as measurement-dependent summaries.

A second motivation is the dynamic nature of participation. A low-activity address can become active, stop transacting, or return after an inactive interval. Annual roles describe the accumulated history; monthly states describe behaviour within shorter windows. Restricting evaluation to addresses active in consecutive months answers a persistence question, but omits disengagement and reactivation. Both views are needed to explain what a temporal result represents.

These needs create a validation problem in the absence of ground-truth behavioural classes. Internal metrics describe separation in the chosen feature space, while seed comparisons assess optimization sensitivity. Neither determines whether the same groups appear under different training samples, whether simple rules capture comparable structure, or whether the labels align with interactions after accounting for activity. A useful evaluation must expose these alternatives rather than selecting whichever metric favours one cluster count.

The layered framework assigns a specific question to each analysis. Annual profiles describe the groups; feature holdouts and equal-family weighting examine representation dependence; resampling and alternative estimators examine fitting sensitivity. Network nulls assess label--endpoint alignment, including the extent to which monthly transfer activity accounts for that alignment. Frozen monthly models examine persistence and conditional return, while inactivity-aware transitions and later-period participation baselines make the conditioning and practical limits visible. Daily time/frequency representations ask which aspects of temporal organization overlap with the aggregate roles.

These layers share addresses and source records, so they are complementary views rather than independent replications. Their purpose is to make the segmentation assessable: which findings survive stronger controls, which are captured by simpler descriptions, and which remain specific to a representation or observation window? This turns the study from a catalogue of clusters into a reproducible examination of the evidence needed to interpret them.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Address Linkage and Behavioural Classification}
Ethereum address analysis includes ownership-linkage heuristics and profiling from transaction histories. Victor investigates address-clustering heuristics \cite{victor2020address}, while B\'{e}res et al. study profiling and deanonymization using graph and behavioural information \cite{beres2021blockchain}. These studies establish that observable histories carry information about addresses and their relationships. Our objective concerns behavioural similarity within a specified population. Similar feature profiles do not establish common ownership, and the resulting segments are not entity-resolution outputs.

Bonifazi et al. represent Ethereum users through multivariate behavioural time series and define user spectra relative to predefined categories \cite{bonifazi2022userspectra}. This perspective motivates treating temporal activity as part of the representation rather than reducing every history to a single total. Our study learns partitions without semantic labels and then compares annual, monthly and signal representations. Consequently, raw-unit profiles, cross-representation agreement and sensitivity analyses must carry the interpretation that externally supplied categories would otherwise provide.

\subsection{Temporal and Network Perspectives}
Temporal graph properties have been used for malicious-account detection \cite{agarwal2021temporal}. T-EDGE represents Ethereum interactions through temporal weighted multidigraph embeddings \cite{tedge2020}, while Liang et al. examine the evolution of cryptocurrency transaction networks \cite{liang2018evolutionary}. These approaches motivate preserving interaction and temporal information when studying blockchain behaviour. Here the network is primarily an evaluation view of activity-derived segments, rather than the input to a graph representation-learning objective.

This distinction creates an important control requirement. Count-based features can correlate with transfer-network activity, so unrestricted label randomization alone does not establish structure beyond activity differences. We therefore compare unrestricted and activity-stratified network nulls and examine sensitivity to repeated pairs, self-transfers, dominant tokens and highly active endpoints. Likewise, temporal organization is evaluated with independently trained monthly models and full-year signal descriptions, whose inferential roles remain separate.

\subsection{Validation of Unsupervised Partitions}
Clustering validation distinguishes geometric compactness, agreement under perturbation and substantive usefulness \cite{halkidi2001validation}. Liu et al. review stability estimation and emphasize the significance of the perturbation being studied \cite{liu2022stability}. We distinguish changes in initialization, training addresses, feature families and estimator geometry. These checks answer different questions and need not support the same resolution.

ARI and NMI compare partitions without relying on arbitrary label numbers \cite{hubert1985partitions,vinh2010information}; Hungarian matching supports address-level agreement and matched cluster comparisons \cite{kuhn1955hungarian}. Silhouette describes internal separation \cite{rousseeuw1987silhouettes}. None supplies ground truth. Our evaluation combines these diagnostics with simple segmentation baselines, declared network randomization families and temporally separated participation comparisons. The intended advance is the controlled assessment of a behavioural segmentation across these views, including explicit reporting when a stronger control weakens its interpretation.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Study Design and Historical Cohort}
The study uses the ERC-20 Trading 2022 release \cite{somin2025erc2022,somin2025dataset}. Its supplied annual feature universe contains 2,738,243 addresses. A complete historical scan queries code at block 16,308,189, the final Ethereum block of 2022: 2,508,997 addresses have exactly empty code and 229,246 have nonempty code, with no unresolved queries. Membership and row order are frozen with checksums. This defines a year-end code-free cohort, not continuous EOA status. An independent audit of all 83,519,492 merged wallet-day rows finds that the supplied candidate set exactly matches addresses with at least ten annual count incidences, with no omitted or additional addresses under this rule. This establishes empirical selection equivalence within the supplied daily source, not reconstruction of raw-transfer cleaning or historical producer intent. The analysis therefore describes an activity-selected population; cluster shares do not estimate prevalence across all Ethereum users.

The same membership supports three descriptions: annual aggregate roles, separately learned monthly states and full-year daily signals (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Raw network processing covers 245,911,059 transfer rows across twelve months. Wallet-day sources are checked for normalized valid addresses, dates within the expected month, unique wallet--day rows, and finite nonnegative integer counts. Counts denote wallet-record incidence, not distinct blockchain transactions: a transfer contributes sender and recipient records, including two incidences for a self-transfer. Daily signal totals reconcile exactly with the annual count source for every retained address.

The diagram separates preparation, representation and evaluation because each stage addresses a different part of the research question. Stage A determines whose activity can enter the analysis. Stage B compresses the year into address-level measurements, providing a readable population summary. Stage C retains the ordering of daily observations, so two addresses with comparable annual totals can still receive different signal descriptions. Stage D asks how much of the resulting structure survives alternative measurements and explicit controls. The final synthesis combines these answers without assuming that agreement across every view is necessary or that disagreement can be ignored.

The downward signal branch is parallel to annual clustering: annual K3 labels are not inputs to the time-domain or frequency-domain fits. Likewise, monthly state models are learned from early-month observations rather than obtained by applying annual cluster numbers to shorter windows. In the validation branch, feature-space checks examine membership sensitivity; network tests examine label alignment with observed interactions; temporal-state tests examine persistence, switching and inactivity; and cross-representation comparisons measure overlap between independently constructed descriptions. The additional participation task asks whether a compact monthly state is useful beyond simple activity measurements. These distinctions give the visual framework an operational meaning and prevent the final synthesis from becoming a count of favourable tests.

\subsection{Annual Features and Partition Robustness}
Sixteen features form three families. Activity level contains observed days, total count, and observed-day count mean, maximum and standard deviation. Persistence/concentration contains inclusive first-to-last span, observed days divided by span, count coefficient of variation, and maximum-day count divided by total count. Directionality contains incoming-, outgoing- and both-active days, their three observed-day ratios, and incoming-minus-outgoing day balance divided by observed days. Directional flags use positive pooled daily incoming/outgoing amounts; zero-value transfers can contribute counts without activating a flag. Amount magnitudes are excluded, but direction flags retain this preprocessing dependence.

Counts and durations receive $\log(1+x)$; ratios, balance, coefficient of variation and concentration remain untransformed. Each column is centred by its cohort median and divided by its interquartile range, using one for a zero range. The annual matrix contains no token-price, gas, identity or legacy-cluster input. Correlated features and seven direction coordinates can implicitly weight related measurements more than once.

MiniBatchKMeans fits K2--K6 using seeds 492--494, ten initializations, batch size 8,192 and 200 maximum iterations. Silhouette uses the same 5,000-address sample at seed 492; seed ARI/NMI and matched agreement use all addresses \cite{hubert1985partitions,vinh2010information,kuhn1955hungarian,rousseeuw1987silhouettes}. K3 feature-family removals retain estimator settings and the original scaling of surviving columns.

Additional retrospective controls fix 50,000 evaluation addresses and exclude them from every new fit. At each K2--K6, thirty fits use independent 100,000-address subsets and twenty use 80\% of the remaining cohort, keeping optimizer seed 492 and the original scaler fixed. Assignments are compared with the original full-cohort reference, which did see those features; this is partition robustness conditional on scaling, not predictive validation. Matched cluster Jaccard scores complement ARI. On one shared 100,000-address subset, K3--K5 also compare batch k-means, diagonal-covariance Gaussian mixtures and equal-family weighting at three seeds. Weighting divides coordinates by the square root of their family's column count. Full settings and all cluster-level results are in the supplementary material.

\subsection{Network Nulls, Baselines and Graph Sensitivity}
For eligible sampled transfer rows $E_m$, with both endpoints in the cohort, annual labels $g$ define
\begin{equation}
H_m=|E_m|^{-1}\sum_{(u,v)\in E_m}\mathbf{1}\{g(u)=g(v)\}.
\end{equation}
The original comparison uses 200,000 shared rows per month and common permutations across seven label solutions: K2--K6 seed 492 and two additional K3 seeds. Labels are shuffled among sampled endpoint wallets while edges remain fixed, including repeated rows and self-transfers. Upper-tail Monte Carlo p-values use $(1+\#\{H_m^{(b)}\ge H_m\})/(B+1)$ with $B=9,999$. Effect size is observed minus null-mean concentration. Holm correction initially covers 84 tests \cite{holm1979sequential}.

The extension compares annual K3--K5 and two simple three-group baselines on common uniform samples of up to 50,000 non-self rows per month. One baseline uses empirical transfer-count terciles, assigning ties together to the upper interval. The other assigns span at most thirty days to one group, and splits remaining addresses at twenty-four observed active days. These illustrative thresholds are fixed without optimizing a network outcome; group sizes are not matched to K3.

Both unrestricted and activity-stratified label permutations are used. Strata combine $\lfloor\log_2(1+d_{\rm in})\rfloor$ and $\lfloor\log_2(1+d_{\rm out})\rfloor$, where counts use all eligible non-self monthly events. Shuffling preserves label composition within sampled-endpoint strata. K3 additionally tests all events, unique directed non-self pairs, exclusion of the month's most frequent eligible token, and exclusion of the top 1\% endpoints ranked by eligible non-self event activity. Deduplication and exclusions precede sampling. The 216 extension tests use 9,999 permutations each; we also correct them jointly with the original 84 tests as a 300-test family. Approximate activity strata do not control exact degree or all token exposure; changed graph populations prevent causal interpretations of sensitivity differences.

\subsection{Monthly States, Inactivity and Participation}
Monthly models use transactions per calendar day, active-day fraction, transactions per active day, calendar-day count coefficient of variation, peak-count share and inclusive span fraction. The first, third and fourth features receive $\log(1+x)$ before robust scaling. Up to 20,000 lexicographically sorted and uniformly sampled active observations per January--June month fit a shared scaler and separate K2--K6 models; centroids are frozen for July--December. Models use seed 492, ten initializations, batch size 4,096 and 100 maximum iterations. Wallets can recur in training months.

For five held-out adjacent pairs, active-only persistence $A_o$ is compared with marginal chance $A_e=\sum_c p_t(c)p_{t+1}(c)$ and $\kappa=(A_o-A_e)/(1-A_e)$ \cite{cohen1960agreement}. For four triples, return conditions on activity in all three months and a first-step switch. Future labels are shuffled within middle-state groups, preserving middle-to-future margins among switchers. There are 999 permutations and twenty K2--K6 return tests in one Holm family. Binary K2 is structurally degenerate because switchers' starting states are determined by the middle state, leaving return counts unchanged under this null.

Inactivity is additionally encoded as an observation category after K3 assignment, allowing all-cohort transitions without fitting an inactive centroid. Previously observed inactive addresses are distinguished from those not yet observed active in 2022; this is not an address-creation classification.

A separate next-month participation task learns K2--K6 vocabularies on January--March only, fits prediction models on January--May pairs (up to 50,000 active observations per month), and evaluates July--November pairs. Constant prevalence and smoothed state rates are compared with two-feature activity logistic regression, six-feature logistic regression, its K3-indicator augmentation, and six-feature histogram gradient boosting. No evaluation features or outcomes fit these models. Brier/log loss are pooled by observations, while AUC is averaged over months. Two hundred paired wallet-level Poisson bootstraps preserve repeated-wallet contributions across months for descriptive loss-difference intervals conditional on fitted models. They do not account for network dependence. This remains temporal evaluation within a retrospectively selected cohort, not prospective population-wide prediction.

\subsection{Daily Signals and Reproduction Controls}
All 365 daily counts, including zeros on absent days, are reconstructed for every cohort address. Six time-domain features summarize active fraction, longest active/inactive runs, active/inactive switches, peak share and calendar-day coefficient of variation. Frequency features apply rFFT to within-wallet centred $\log(1+x_t)$, exclude DC and normalize non-DC power. Seven summaries capture power above sixty days, fourteen--sixty days and below fourteen days, weekly/monthly bands, peak-power share and spectral entropy. Weekly and monthly bands use $1/8\le f<1/6$ and $1/35\le f<1/25$ cycles per day. Discrete bins approximate calendar periods. Zero-power signals would be excluded from both partitions; none occur here.

Each representation is robust-scaled separately, logging only count coefficient of variation beforehand. K2--K6 fits at three seeds use a shared uniform 100,000-address training sample, ten initializations, batch size 8,192 and 200 maximum iterations; assignments cover the full eligible cohort and silhouette uses 3,000 fixed addresses. Seventy-five comparisons measure annual--signal and time--frequency overlap. These are full-year descriptive fits.

A uniform 2,000-address sample tests sample-mean low-frequency share, peak-power share and negative entropy against histogram-preserving nulls. Complete daily permutation uses 999 replicates and a three-test Holm family. Additional seven- and thirty-day block permutations retain within-block order after a random circular origin, preserving the shorter final block. The three null types form a nine-test extension family. They test temporal ordering, not recurring cycles or individual-wallet significance.

Feature and matrix hashes reproduce exactly. Thirteen of fifteen annual label arrays match archived hashes; K3 seed 494 and K6 seed 493 are identified as reproduced fits under the rerun runtime. Current tables use the complete rerun. Manifests record source identity, row order, settings, samples and result hashes. All analyses are retrospective; the complementary views share source data and are not independent replications.

\subsection{Evaluation Questions and Interpretation}
The evaluation follows four connected questions. First, can a small annual vocabulary describe differences that a transfer total conceals? Raw-unit profiles address readability, while silhouette and initialization comparisons describe the fitted geometry. Second, does membership survive perturbations that matter for interpretation? Training-subset refits, family removals and estimator comparisons deliberately perturb different parts of the construction. Third, do the descriptions align with interaction and temporal patterns under appropriate conditioning? Network randomization, frozen monthly models and timing surrogates each specify what remains fixed when an observed result is compared with chance. Fourth, does the vocabulary improve a concrete task beyond transparent alternatives? The participation comparison supplies a limited, measurable answer to this practical question.

No single score answers all four questions. A high ARI across seeds means that an optimizer repeatedly finds similar assignments under the declared inputs; it does not imply invariance to a different sample or representation. A positive network excess means more same-label concentration than the selected null expects; changing what the null preserves changes the claim. Above-chance monthly persistence concerns the addresses observed in both months, whereas participation prediction asks whether they will be active again. Finally, signal overlap measures correspondence between descriptions, while a timing permutation tests the organization of counts within each signal. Reporting these quantities together makes the reasoning behind the behavioural interpretation explicit.

There is also a substantive difference between repeated transfers and distinct relationships. Event sampling gives more influence to address pairs that transact repeatedly, whereas the unique-pair variant gives each retained directed pair one contribution before sampling. Dominant-token exclusion asks whether a pooled result depends on one transfer environment, while endpoint exclusion asks how the retained population affects alignment. These are complementary sensitivity questions, each with its own denominator. Their disagreement is informative about what the aggregate graph result summarizes.

The simple baselines sharpen this interpretation. Count terciles show greater unrestricted excess than annual K3 on the common samples, even though they use a single activity quantity. Their advantage disappears under the stratified test. This does not make count terciles a superior behavioural taxonomy; it demonstrates why unrestricted graph alignment alone cannot establish the added value of a multivariate segmentation. The span/activity rule is closer to K3 in full-cohort ARI, yet its controlled graph support is also limited. Together, these controls connect the original descriptive objective to the possibility that a simpler representation explains part of the observed structure.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{From Transaction Histories to a Population Description}
The motivating problem is not a lack of observable records, but the difficulty of interpreting them collectively. The annual profiles illustrate what a compact vocabulary contributes: two lower-count groups have the same median count yet sharply different participation spans, while sustained activity identifies a more extended pattern. A single total would combine these histories. The temporal views then show why a year-level description is incomplete: current activity can continue, stop or resume, and the daily arrangement contains information that annual accumulation removes. The framework makes these distinctions available within one explicitly defined population.

This contribution is descriptive before it is operational. An analyst can use the profiles to formulate questions about short episodes, sustained participation or changing state composition. Moving from those questions to anomaly detection, simulation or targeted interventions would require a task-specific evaluation and independently justified outcomes. The participation experiment provides one such bounded check and prevents a readable segmentation from being treated as evidence of broad practical superiority. Its result is useful precisely because it separates compression of behaviour from improvement over the activity variables being compressed.

\subsection{What the Annual Segmentation Can Summarize}
The K3 profiles distinguish sustained activity, distributed lower activity and short-span concentration within this representation. Those contrasts are useful descriptions, but strong agreement across three optimizer seeds is narrower than stability to training-address selection and induced input order. Resampling, feature removal and estimator comparisons show that the partition is not invariant. Simple rules also capture part of its structure. Accordingly, K3 is retained as an interpretable reference vocabulary rather than a uniquely validated behavioural taxonomy.

\subsection{Why Network Controls Change the Interpretation}
Activity-derived labels can correlate with endpoint activity. Unrestricted permutations break that association, whereas the stratified null retains part of it. The controlled comparison in Table~\ref{tab:conditionednetwork} therefore tests a stronger question than the original positive network result. Loss of support in most months narrows the unrestricted interpretation, while support after hub exclusion shows that the relation depends on the retained interaction population (Table~\ref{tab:graphsensitivity}). Neither result implies that all label--network relationships are absent or isolates a causal hub effect. Negative excess is descriptive under the upper-tail test, not a confirmed lower-tail result. Logarithmic bins, shared token exposure and within-bin heterogeneity still limit adjustment.

\subsection{Temporal Structure and Practical Usefulness}
Annual roles, signal partitions and monthly states have different meanings. Active-only persistence omits inactivity and reactivation, while conditional return further selects first-step switchers. K3 return excess is approximately two percentage points despite corrected significance. In the separate prediction task, state-only rates improve on constant prevalence but do not outperform activity-feature baselines; K3 indicators add only small, metric-dependent changes. The study therefore demonstrates temporal organization without establishing predictive superiority of the segmentation. Block-null timing effects concern arrangement beyond partially preserved local episodes, not recurring cycles or human behavioural mechanisms.

\subsection{How the Validation Layers Fit Together}
The layers support a qualified answer to the research question. Annual K3 is readable and repeats well across the original initializations, but its sensitivity to training subsets and feature families limits claims of invariant membership. Network alignment is visible under an unrestricted shuffle, but activity conditioning removes most monthly support in the main non-self comparison. Monthly persistence and return reveal structured movement within selected active populations, while inactivity changes the population being described. Signal comparisons reveal partial correspondence and temporal ordering under declared surrogates. Each result contributes an answer with a different scope; combining them does not erase those scopes.

This also explains why the final synthesis in Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} is an interpretation rather than a new fitted classifier. There is no learned fusion score that turns the annual, monthly, network and signal outputs into verified identities. Agreement can strengthen a particular descriptive claim, while disagreement identifies the conditions under which that claim fails. For example, stronger support after endpoint exclusion locates a graph-dependent association, but does not repair annual membership sensitivity or establish signal periodicity. Keeping those conclusions separate makes the integrated framework more useful for designing the next empirical test.

\subsection{Population Scope and Reproducibility}
The cohort is conditional on the observed at-least-ten-count candidate rule and one year-end code observation, not every Ethereum user or continuous EOA identity. Counts are record incidences, and amount-derived directional flags retain preprocessing limitations. Data shared across validation views prevent interpreting them as independent replications. Address-level resampling and bootstrap intervals do not capture all network dependence, cohort-selection uncertainty or between-year variation. The documented controls improve auditability, but additional years, independent raw-transfer reconstruction and independently labelled outcomes remain necessary for broader social-system interpretation. Reproduction checks establish traceability of measurements and summaries, not semantic ground truth.

\section{Conclusion}
This study treats Ethereum wallet segmentation as a behavioural description requiring explicit validation. A complete historical scan defines 2,508,997 year-end code-free addresses, and a sixteen-feature count/activity/direction representation yields an interpretable annual K3 working summary. Its strong original seed agreement is qualified by training-subset variability, feature dependence and disagreement with alternative estimator geometry. Unrestricted network alignment does not by itself establish structure beyond activity; the stratified null and simple baselines materially narrow that interpretation.

Monthly persistence, inactivity, conditional return and later-period participation describe different aspects of changing activity. Prediction baselines prevent an interpretable state vocabulary from being mistaken for demonstrated predictive superiority. Rebuilt daily signals add cross-representation and block-null timing comparisons without identifying permanent rhythms. The resulting framework makes both support and limitations reproducible. Its empirical contribution concerns observable participation within one defined ERC-20 population and year; broader social, economic and cross-year interpretations require additional evidence.
